Postdoctoral researchers

Establish a new research area within the lab.

How to join

  1. Review our current and prior research projects and review our publications to determine if the Gabbard Lab is the right fit for you.
  2. Email Chase (chase.gabbard@me.gatech.edu) with your CV, a one-page summary of the research you would like to pursue, and the email addresses of two professional references.
  3. If you plan to apply for a fellowship with the Gabbard Lab as your host, get in touch at least two months before the deadline so we can develop the proposal together.

Fellowships and opportunities

National fellowships2 programs

  • NSF Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Research Fellowship (MSPRF)

    Supports postdoctoral research in the mathematical sciences, including applications to other disciplines such as fluid mechanics.

    Eligibility
    U.S. citizens, nationals, and permanent residents who earned a PhD within the past two years
    Award
    $190,000 in total, including a stipend of up to $70,000 a year
    Deadline
    Third Wednesday in October each year

  • Schmidt Science Fellows

    For scientists who want to pivot to a new field after the PhD. Fellows choose a host lab anywhere in the world.

    Eligibility
    PhD graduates in the natural sciences, engineering, mathematics, or computing, nominated by their PhD institution
    Award
    $110,000 a year for up to two years
    Deadline
    Set by each nominating university, typically in the spring
International fellowships4 programs

Researchers from outside the United States can bring a fellowship from home. Common options include:

  • MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships (Global Fellowships)

    European Union fellowship for 12 to 24 months at a host outside Europe, followed by a 12-month return phase in Europe.

    Eligibility
    European nationals and long-term residents
    Deadline
    Annual call, usually closing in September

  • DFG Walter Benjamin Programme

    German Research Foundation funding for early-career postdocs, which can be held as a fellowship at a research institution abroad.

    Eligibility
    Early-career postdocs with ties to the German research system
    Deadline
    Proposals accepted at any time

  • SNSF Postdoc.Mobility

    Swiss National Science Foundation fellowship for a 24-month research stay abroad, with an optional return phase in Switzerland.

    Eligibility
    Swiss citizens and residents, or PhD graduates of Swiss institutions, within three years of the doctorate
    Deadline
    Early February and early August each year

  • JSPS Overseas Research Fellowships

    Two years of research at a university outside Japan.

    Eligibility
    Japanese nationals and permanent residents of Japan within five years of the PhD
    Award
    Round-trip airfare and a living allowance of ¥5.2–8 million a year

Graduate students

Learn to conduct, communicate, and lead research.

How to join

  1. Review our open positions, then apply to the PhD program in the George W. Woodruff School of Mechanical Engineering and mention the Gabbard Lab in your application.
  2. Email Chase (chase.gabbard@me.gatech.edu) with your CV, a few sentences about the research you would like to do, and the email addresses of two professional references.
  3. Apply for fellowships early. Many national fellowships close in October, the fall before you would start.

Fellowships and opportunities

Fellowships at Georgia Tech4 programs

  • Stamps Fellows Program

    Georgia Tech’s most prestigious merit-based doctoral fellowship, for students with outstanding academic records and leadership potential.

    A supplemental award from the ARCS Foundation for outstanding PhD students in science and engineering, including mechanical engineering.

    Eligibility

National fellowships6 programs

  • NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)

    Three years of NSF support for research-based graduate study in science and engineering.

    Georgia Tech application support

    Eligibility
    U.S. citizens, nationals, and permanent residents who are college seniors, graduates not yet in graduate school, or first-year graduate students
    Award
    $37,000 a year plus a $16,000 education allowance, for three years
    Deadline
    Mid-to-late October; applications open in the summer

  • National Defense Science and Engineering Graduate (NDSEG) Fellowship

    Department of Defense fellowship for doctoral study in science and engineering fields of importance to national defense.

    Eligibility
    U.S. citizens and nationals who are seniors or in their first two years of graduate study
    Award
    Full tuition and a stipend for three years
    Deadline
    Late October; applications open in August

  • Hertz Fellowship

    Support for PhD students in the applied physical and biological sciences, mathematics, and engineering.

    Eligibility
    U.S. citizens and permanent residents applying before or during the first year of a PhD
    Award
    Stipend and full tuition for up to five years, valued at up to $250,000
    Deadline
    Late October

  • Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans

    For immigrants and the children of immigrants pursuing graduate study in any field.

    Eligibility
    Age 30 or younger, before or within the first two years of graduate school
    Award
    Up to $90,000 over two years toward stipend and tuition
    Deadline
    Late October

  • SMART Scholarship-for-Service

    Full tuition and a stipend, followed by civilian work at a U.S. defense research facility, one year for each year of support.

    Eligibility
    Citizens of the U.S., Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the U.K. with a GPA of 3.0 or higher; undergraduates may also apply
    Award
    Full tuition, a $30,000–$46,000 annual stipend, and paid summer internships
    Deadline
    Early December; applications open August 1

  • DOE Office of Science Graduate Student Research (SCGSR)

    A supplemental award to carry out part of your PhD research at a Department of Energy national laboratory.

    Eligibility
    U.S. citizens and permanent residents who have reached PhD candidacy, in fields that support the Office of Science mission
    Award
    Living stipend and travel for a stay of 3 to 12 months
    Deadline
    Varies by year; check the current call

Applying to the PhD program

How to apply

Program
PhD in Mechanical Engineering, George W. Woodruff School of Mechanical Engineering
Deadlines
December 15 for fall entry and October 1 for spring entry, by 11:59 p.m. Eastern
Minimums
A four-year bachelor’s degree or equivalent and a GPA of at least 3.3
English
For applicants who need to show proficiency: a TOEFL iBT total of 4.5 (94 on tests taken before January 2026) or an IELTS Academic score of 7, with section minimums
Materials
Transcripts from each institution, up to three letters of recommendation, and program questions such as your research experience and the faculty you would like to work with
Fee waivers
Available to many applicants, including first-generation and low-income U.S. applicants

Application timeline

For a fall start. Starting in the spring instead? The deadline is October 1.

  1. Summer to early fall

    Find your fit

    Review our research and publications, then email Chase (chase.gabbard@me.gatech.edu) with your CV, research interests, and the email addresses of two professional references.

  2. September and October

    Prepare your application

    Ask your recommenders early and gather transcripts from each institution you attended.

  3. October

    Apply for national fellowships

    The NSF GRFP closes in mid-to-late October, and the NDSEG and Hertz fellowships in late October.

  4. December 15

    Submit your application

    Apply to Georgia Tech by 11:59 p.m. Eastern for fall entry, and answer the Stamps Fellows essay in the same application.

  5. Spring

    Hear back

    Admission decisions are posted in Georgia Tech’s applicant portal.

  6. August

    Start your PhD

    Join the lab at the beginning of the fall semester.

Fellowships and opportunities

Ways to do research at Georgia Tech4 options

  • Research for credit

    • ME 2699
    • ME 4699
    • ME 4903

    Earn a letter grade for research: ME 2699 for first- and second-year students and ME 4699 for third- and fourth-year students. ME 4903 Special Problems covers individual study, including design projects. Plan on about three hours a week per credit hour; up to six credits can count as free electives.

  • Volunteer research

    • ME 4698
    • No credit or pay

    Join the lab on a flexible schedule without credit or pay. There is no grade, but your research is recorded on your transcript.

  • Paid research

    • ME 4698
    • Hourly wage

    Earn an hourly wage for your research through a PURA Salary Award or, when positions are available, through the lab. Paid research does not earn course credit.

  • Vertically Integrated Projects

    • 1–3 credit hours
    • Multiple semesters

    Team-based research for credit that can continue for several semesters. Open to sophomores, juniors, and seniors through the VIP program; first-year students can apply in the spring to join as sophomores.

Registering. All Woodruff School research courses require a permit. Agree on a project with Chase, then submit the ME research permit request by 11:59 p.m. on the Thursday of the first week of classes.

    Undergraduates in good standing with a Georgia Tech faculty mentor; cannot be combined with course credit for the same project

    Funding to present your research at a conference.

    Eligibility
    Undergraduates whose talk or poster has been accepted

    Up to $1,000
    Deadline
    Rolling; apply one to three months before the conference

    Build a multi-semester project into an undergraduate thesis.

    Requirements
    At least nine credit hours of research over two or more terms, for pay or credit, plus the research writing courses LMC 4701 and 4702

  • NASA Georgia Space Grant Consortium

    NASA-funded fellowships for Georgia Tech students advancing science and engineering.

    Eligibility

Visiting scholars

Collaborate on a focused project during a research stay.

How to join

  1. Email Chase (chase.gabbard@me.gatech.edu) with your CV, a short project proposal, your preferred dates, and your source of funding.
  2. Once we agree on a project, Georgia Tech will issue an invitation. International visitors are sponsored for a J-1 exchange visitor visa, which requires proof of English proficiency and of funding for the full stay.
  3. Start planning several months before your intended arrival to allow time for visa processing.

Fellowships and opportunities

Funding your visit3 programs

Most visitors are supported by their home institution or by a fellowship. We are glad to support applications that name the Gabbard Lab as host.

  • Fulbright Visiting Scholar Program

    Three to twelve months of research or teaching at a U.S. institution.

    Eligibility
    Citizens of participating countries who hold a doctorate; not open to U.S. citizens or permanent residents
    How to apply
    Through the Fulbright Commission or U.S. Embassy in your home country

  • Fulbright Foreign Student Program

    Graduate study and research in the United States.

    Eligibility
    Graduate students and young professionals from participating countries
    How to apply
    Through the Fulbright Commission or U.S. Embassy in your home country

  • China Scholarship Council

    Funds research stays abroad for students and scholars from China, including visits of 6 to 24 months by PhD students.

    Eligibility
    Students and scholars at institutions in China, with an invitation from the host
    Award
    Monthly living stipend

Also see. Postdocs can bring fellowships such as MSCA, DFG, SNSF, and JSPS (see postdoctoral researchers), and undergraduates from other universities can visit through SURE or DAAD RISE (see undergraduate students).
